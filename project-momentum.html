Momentum

Overview

The first ever game that I worked on — a 3D platformer built from scratch in Unity, with jumping, wall running, grappling, and secret areas hidden through the levels.

Momentum is set inside a volcano, players take the role of Craig's soul, in a helpless bid to escape the torment of the hell he found himself in due to the deeds he committed in life. If you hit an obstacle, or the floor, you have to start all the way from the beginning of the level, and i've seen people who play the game take at least 10 minues to beat a level, but getting better each run.

Design & build

I built the platforming systems myself — jumping, wall running, and grappling all needed to feel tight and responsive for a game built around precision movement.

To build the levels, I began with a rough sketch, planning where I would want things, I spent most of my time whiteboxing, making sure each level flows properly, but is still difficult to complete. I wanted each level to be slightly longer and more difficult than the last. I did this through introducing new levels and different mechanics for the player, such as a grappling hook. At the point where it took me and playtesters some time to complete, i'd texture everything and make sure everything still flowed correctly, then the level was done.

Challenges & solutions

I was working with a mediocre laptop at the time, so my optimisation skills had to be off the charts — every system needed to run smoothly without expensive hardware to fall back on.

To optimise the levels, I made sure that each level had detail, but it was kept to a minimum to make sure even laptops of a bad quality could still run it. As it was my first project, doing this helped me learn for the rest of my games how to make them work well.

What I learned

Playtesting taught me that I'm never going to get it right on the first try, because people will always find bugs you didn't expect.

A player got hit by one of the obstacles and it sent them flying away. I had to repair this by turning the knockback down slightly, because where I had it was clearly too intense. As I introduced each level, i made sure to thoroughly play through them, looking for bugs, but it got to a point where I couldn't find any so I gave them to a small group of testers and they found more bugs.
